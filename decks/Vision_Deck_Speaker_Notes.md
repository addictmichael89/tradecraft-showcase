We did not build this in a corporate office, and we did not build it as a toy for paper pushers. It was forged on bare metal inside The Fortress v1.26 to take next-generation AI out of theoretical ivory towers and place it straight into the hands of working tradesmen worldwide.

### Slide 5
Our entire engineering roadmap rests on three unbreakable pillars.

Pillar One is the Intelligent Curriculum OS. It’s the brain. It takes standards from NCCER, NEC, or EPA, indexes them into a sovereign vector database, and generates exams, rubric-aligned labs, and personalized AI tutoring.

Pillar Two is Zero-IT Local Hardware. We ship a rugged, pre-configured box. You plug in power and an ethernet cable. It powers the whole campus locally with dual redundancy. There are no AWS bills, no OpenAI API fees, and no waiting on external servers.

Pillar Three is Edge-to-Toolbox Continuity. When you’ve worked in the field, you know that the moment an apprentice graduates, they are suddenly cut off from school resources. We built TradeCraft so the software doesn't stay behind on a classroom desk. It lives on their phone and truck tablet, assisting them through load calculations, junction box fill, and motor branch circuits on real jobsites.

### Slide 6
Let’s address the elephant in the room when it comes to educational technology: support overhead. The average trade school does not have an enterprise IT department. If a cloud software package requires active directory integrations, specialized firewall tunnels, or constant maintenance, it ends up abandoned.

TradeCraft OS was designed with an absolute zero-IT mandate. The hardware appliance ships pre-loaded with the school's curriculum, local LLMs, vector memory, and grading pipelines. It auto-discovers the network, pairs with existing office multifunction printers for scan-to-grade, and serves student laptops or tablets locally.

Because it runs 100% locally, the school pays zero cloud token fees. An instructor can grade 10,000 exams and have 500 students chat with Study Buddy all day long—the marginal cost to the school is exactly zero dollars. That is an unbeatable economic moat.

### Slide 8
Now let’s look at what we are building in our R&D lab: Spatial Lab Assistants.

A chatbot on a screen is fine for answering text questions, but the physical trades do not live in text boxes—they live in 3D physical space. An electrical apprentice needs to know where to bend a piece of EMT conduit, how to route around a water line, and whether their junction box meets NEC box-fill volume requirements.

With our Spatial Lab Assistant, the student snaps a photo of their physical workbench. The vision model identifies the panel, the conduit, and the conductors. It projects 3D spatial vectors directly over the image, showing the student exactly why a bend needs to be made at 30 degrees, calculating the take-up and shrink, and citing the exact NEC code article.

It turns every single smartphone into a patient, tireless master craftsman looking over the apprentice's shoulder.

### Slide 9
As we move from the classroom into the field, we are strapping eyes, ears, and physical sensors onto our AI hardware.

When a technician arrives at a commercial jobsite today, they carry multiple expensive, disconnected handheld tools: an infrared thermal camera, an ultrasonic leak detector, a laser measure, and a multimeter.

We are integrating multi-modal sensor fusion directly into the TradeCraft hardware substrate. Imagine a ruggedized handheld node or clip-on sensor array: 3D LiDAR creates an instant point-cloud of the utility room to plan pipe runs; thermal mapping detects an overloaded phase behind a deadfront panel before the technician even touches it; acoustic sensing hears the high-frequency hiss of an arc-flash or a 5-micron refrigerant leak.

This transforms TradeCraft from a software system into the ultimate jobsite diagnostic instrument—protecting the tradesman's life and saving commercial contractors tens of thousands of dollars in diagnostic downtime.
